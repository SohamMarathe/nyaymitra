## procedures/

Source: https://cybercrime.gov.in — the National Cyber Crime Reporting
Portal (I4C, Ministry of Home Affairs). It has clear citizen-facing guidance
on reporting financial fraud, identity theft, and harassment.

Suggested file: `cybercrime_reporting_procedure.txt` — cover:
- Call **1930** (National Cyber Crime Helpline) within the first hour for
  financial fraud — this is the single most important actionable fact for
  this domain, since fast reporting materially improves fund-recovery odds
- File a detailed written complaint at cybercrime.gov.in within 24 hours
- What evidence to gather (screenshots, transaction IDs, headers)

## judgments/

https://indiankanoon.org — search "IT Act phishing judgment" or "identity
theft Section 66C". Real starting points, verified:

- **Shreya Singhal v. Union of India** (2015) 5 SCC 1 — struck down
  Section 66A of the IT Act as unconstitutional; essential context since
  people online still sometimes reference the now-void Section 66A
- **National Association of Software and Service Companies (NASSCOM) v.
  Ajay Sood & Others** — the first Indian case to declare phishing an
  illegal act, allowing injunctions and damages
- **Sharat Babu Digumarti v. Govt. of NCT of Delhi** (2017) 2 SCC 18 —
  held that IT Act Sections 67–67B are a complete code for electronic
  obscenity offences
- **Syed Asifuddin & Ors. v. State of Andhra Pradesh** — tampering with
  computer source documents (Section 65), arose from a mobile-phone
  ESN-manipulation case
- The 2005 **Mphasis BPO data theft case** — early, widely-cited example
  of Section 43(a) (unauthorized access) applied to a bank-employee fraud
